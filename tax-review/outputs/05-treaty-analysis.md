## 0. Scope, method and evidence note

**No client source documents exist.** This memo is a **current-law mapping only**: it
identifies which of three bilateral double tax agreements (DTAs) governs each income
flow under each possible residency outcome, and where the three bilaterals leave gaps.
Facts about Theo are used **only** as labelled hypotheticals or flagged gaps; nothing
here establishes a residency conclusion or a tax figure. Residency determination is the
work of the day-count and domestic-law agents; this memo takes the two possible outcomes
(NZ-resident / Thai-resident) as **inputs** and maps the treaty consequences of each.

**Verification method:** every legal claim was checked by web search in **July 2026**
(searches run 10 July 2026). Direct machine-fetch of several primary government texts
(NZ Legislation, AustLII, Thai Revenue Department, ATO/Treasury) was **blocked by the
session egress policy** (HTTP 403 at the proxy). Where a specific **article number** for
the NZ–Thailand DTA could not be read line-by-line from the primary text, it is given
from the **OECD-Model standard structure** that the 1998 treaty follows and labelled
**INFERRED — verify against primary text (DLM267900)**. Treaty **existence, in-force
status, and the article numbers that search results directly confirmed** are labelled
**KNOWN**. Advisers must confirm every article number against the primary text before
relying on it.

**Australian English used throughout.**

## 1. Are the three treaties in force? (verified July 2026)

### 1.1 New Zealand – Thailand DTA — IN FORCE (KNOWN)
- Instrument: **Double Taxation Relief (Thailand) Order 1998 (SR 1998/424)**, scheduling
  the *Agreement between the Government of New Zealand and the Government of the Kingdom
  of Thailand for the avoidance of double taxation…with respect to taxes on income*.
- Signed 1998; effective in NZ from **1 January 1999** (withholding taxes) and the
  **1999–2000 income year** (other income tax). Still current on the NZ IRD tax-treaties
  register at July 2026.
- Sources: NZ Legislation DLM267900; NZ IRD Tax Policy — Thailand; Thai Revenue
  Department (New Zealand treaty pages). URLs in §8.

### 1.2 Australia – Thailand DTA — IN FORCE (KNOWN)
- Instrument: *Agreement between Australia and the Kingdom of Thailand for the Avoidance
  of Double Taxation…with respect to Taxes on Income* — **[1989] ATS 36**, given force of
  law as **Schedule 30 to the International Tax Agreements Act 1953 (Cth)**.
- Signed and entered into force **1989**. **Modified by the MLI**; the ATO published the
  **synthesised (MLI-integrated) text on 19 April 2024**. In force at July 2026.
- Sources: AustLII [1989] ATS 36; ATO income-tax-treaties register; Thai Revenue
  Department (Australia treaty pages). URLs in §8.

### 1.3 New Zealand – Australia DTA (2009) — IN FORCE (KNOWN)
- Instrument: *Convention between Australia and New Zealand for the Avoidance of Double
  Taxation with respect to Taxes on Income and Fringe Benefits…*, **signed in Paris on
  26 June 2009** (Australia: **[2010] ATS 10**). It **replaced** the 1995 Agreement and
  the 2005 Protocol.
- **Modified by the MLI** (e.g. the Article 4 dual-resident-entity rule); ATO publishes a
  synthesised text. In force at July 2026.
- Sources: NZ IRD Tax Policy — Australia (2009 DTA PDF); Australian Treasury tax-treaties;
  ATO MLI synthesised text (newzealand.pdf). URLs in §8.

**There is NO tri-lateral treaty.** All three instruments are strictly bilateral. Their
interaction for a person with ties to all three states is a matter of applying each
bilateral separately (see §5–§6).

## 2. NZ–Thailand DTA — relevant articles

Structure follows the OECD Model of the period. Article numbers marked **INFERRED** must
be verified against DLM267900 (primary text was egress-blocked in this session).

| Topic | Article | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Residence / fiscal domicile + individual tie-breaker | **Art 4** | KNOWN (standard) |
| Permanent establishment | Art 5 | INFERRED (OECD-Model standard) |
| Business profits | **Art 7** | INFERRED (OECD-Model standard) |
| Dividends (WHT limit **15%**) | **Art 10** | Rate KNOWN (IRD release); article INFERRED |
| Interest (WHT limit **10%**) | **Art 11** | Rate KNOWN (IRD release); article INFERRED |
| Royalties (10% / 15%) | Art 12 | KNOWN (IRD release) |
| **Independent personal services (fixed base)** | **Art 14** | INFERRED — this is a **pre-2000 treaty so it RETAINS a separate IPS article** |
| Dependent personal services (employment, 183-day rule) | Art 15 | INFERRED |
| Directors' fees | **Art 16** | INFERRED |
| Elimination of double taxation (credit method) | Art 23/24 | INFERRED |
| Mutual agreement procedure (MAP) | Art 25 | INFERRED |

- **Art 4 tie-breaker cascade (individuals)** — standard OECD sequence: (1) permanent
  home available; (2) if in both, **centre of vital interests** (closer personal and
  economic relations); (3) if unresolved, **habitual abode**; (4) if in both/neither,
  **nationality**; (5) if still unresolved, **mutual agreement** between the competent
  authorities. KNOWN as the cascade; exact wording to be verified against DLM267900.
- **Function of this treaty in Theo's fact pattern:** it governs (a) the **NZ-vs-Thailand
  residence tie-break** (Art 4) and (b) any **NZ-source income taxed while Theo is
  Thai-resident** (or Thai-source income while NZ-resident) and the **relief** the
  residence country must give. On the facts as summarised, Theo's income is **Australian-
  payer income**, so this treaty is unlikely to *govern any income flow directly*; its
  decisive role is the tie-breaker (§4).

## 3. Australia–Thailand DTA (1989) and NZ–Australia DTA (2009) — relevant articles

### 3.1 Australia–Thailand DTA (1989) — an **older, Article-14-retaining** treaty

| Topic | Article | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Residence + individual tie-breaker | **Art 4** | KNOWN (structure); wording to verify |
| Permanent establishment | Art 5 | INFERRED |
| Business profits | **Art 7** | KNOWN (confirmed present) |
| Dividends (source WHT — **15% for ≥25% corporate holding, otherwise up to 20%**) | **Art 10** | Rates KNOWN (secondary); verify tiers |
| Interest (source WHT — **10% to a financial institution, otherwise up to 25%**) | **Art 11** | Rates KNOWN (secondary); verify tiers |
| **Independent personal services (professional services; fixed base / 183-day test)** | **Art 14** | KNOWN — separate IPS article **retained** |
| Dependent personal services | Art 15 | INFERRED |
| **Directors' fees** — company's state of residence **may tax** | **Art 16** | KNOWN (confirmed) |
| Elimination of double taxation | Art 23/25 | INFERRED |
| MAP | Art 24/25 | INFERRED |

- **Art 14 (IPS)**: professional/independent services are taxable in the **residence
  state (Thailand)** unless the individual has a **fixed base regularly available** in the
  source state (Australia), or is present in Australia beyond the treaty day threshold
  (commonly 183 days), in which case Australia may tax the income **attributable to that
  fixed base / presence**. KNOWN that a separate IPS article exists; exact fixed-base and
  day wording to be verified.
- **Art 16 (Directors' fees)**: fees derived by a resident of one state as a board member
  of a company resident in the **other** state **may be taxed in that other state**
  (i.e. Australia may tax fees from an Australian-resident company). KNOWN.

### 3.2 NZ–Australia DTA (2009) — a **modern, Article-14-removed** treaty

| Topic | Article | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Residence + individual tie-breaker (MLI-modified for entities) | **Art 4** | KNOWN |
| Permanent establishment | Art 5 | INFERRED (OECD-Model standard) |
| **Business profits — absorbs independent services** | **Art 7** | KNOWN |
| Dividends (source WHT capped; nil/5%/15% tiers depending on holding) | **Art 10** | KNOWN (article); verify exact tiers |
| Interest | Art 11 | INFERRED |
| **Income from employment (183-day rule)** | **Art 14** | KNOWN (this treaty uses Art 14 for *employment*, NOT IPS) |
| **Directors' fees** | **Art 15** | KNOWN (structure) |
| Elimination of double taxation | Art 23 | INFERRED |
| MAP | Art 25 | INFERRED |

- **No separate Independent Personal Services article.** The 2009 Convention **deleted
  the former Article 14 (Independent Personal Services)**, following the OECD's 2000
  removal of Model Art 14. **Independent/professional services are now dealt with under
  Art 7 (Business Profits)** and taxed only in the residence state unless carried on
  through a **permanent establishment** in the source state. KNOWN.
- **Consequence for Theo:** the **threshold for Australia to tax his contractor fees
  differs by residence.** Under the **2009 (NZ) treaty** the test is a **PE** (Art 5/7).
  Under the **1989 (Thai) treaty** the test is a **fixed base / day-count** under **Art 14
  IPS**. These are not identical tests — a substantive difference, not a formality (§6.4).

## 4. Residence tie-breaker: NZ vs Thailand (the pivot)

If Theo is a resident of **both** NZ and Thailand under their **domestic** laws, the
**NZ–Thailand DTA Art 4** cascade decides his single treaty-residence *as between those
two states*. This is the analytical pivot because it determines **which counterparty
treaty (2009-NZ or 1989-Thai) frames his Australian income** (see §5).

**Cascade applied to Theo (all steps fact-gated — see gaps):**

1. **Permanent home available.** **GAP (pivotal).** Need: does Theo retain a dwelling in
   NZ that is available to him (owned/leased/family home/stored belongings — intake items
   4, 6)? If his **only** available permanent home is the Phuket lease (Sivana Place,
   intake item 8), the first tie-breaker resolves to **Thailand** and the cascade stops.
   If a NZ home remains available, go to step 2.
2. **Centre of vital interests** (closer personal + economic relations). **GAP.**
   - Personal relations: living in Phuket on a DTV since ~May 2025 (INFERRED from brief).
   - Economic relations: **NZ-registered sole trader**, NZ bank account(s), invoicing
     **Australian** companies. Economic ties point partly to NZ (registration/banking)
     and partly to Australia (customers) — **not clearly Thailand**. This step can be
     genuinely indeterminate and is document-hungry (intake items 4–7, 12–16).
3. **Habitual abode.** If vital interests are unclear, habitual abode currently appears to
   be **Thailand** (ASSUMED >180 days in Thailand in 2025 — the brief flags this as the
   pivotal unproven fact; intake §4 A1).
4. **Nationality.** Theo is a **New Zealand** national → this step, if reached, favours
   **NZ**.
5. **MAP.** Competent-authority resolution if all else fails.

**Likely outcome (INFERRED, strictly fact-dependent):** if Theo has genuinely relocated
and holds no available NZ dwelling, steps 1/3 point to **Thai treaty-residence**. If a NZ
home remains available and vital interests are split, the tie-break can run to
**nationality (NZ)** or MAP. **The decisive facts are (a) availability of a NZ permanent
home and (b) the centre-of-vital-interests split — both are current gaps.** Note the
separate, sticky NZ domestic "permanent place of abode" test can keep Theo NZ-resident
under domestic law even if the treaty tie-breaks to Thailand; that is exactly the
dual-resident scenario the tie-breaker exists to resolve.

## 5. How the three bilaterals interact (the triangular mechanic)

Key principle: **for any income flow, the governing treaty is the bilateral between the
SOURCE state and Theo's RESIDENCE state.** The income here is **Australian-payer /
Australian-source** contractor income (plus any AU directors' fees or dividends).
Therefore:

- **If Theo is (treaty-)resident of New Zealand** → his Australian income is framed by the
  **NZ–Australia 2009 DTA**. The NZ–Thailand DTA is not engaged for that flow (Thailand is
  not the residence or the source state for it).
- **If Theo is (treaty-)resident of Thailand** → his Australian income is framed by the
  **Australia–Thailand 1989 DTA**. The NZ–Australia DTA is not engaged for that flow.
- **The NZ–Thailand DTA never directly governs the Australian income** (Australia is not a
  party). Its jobs are: settle NZ-vs-Thai residence (§4) and allocate any NZ-source or
  Thai-source flows and their relief.

**Why the triangle creates risk (no trilateral instrument):**
- Australia is **not bound by** the NZ–Thai tie-break. Australia decides which of *its*
  treaties applies by asking, under each treaty's own Art 4, whether Theo is "a resident
  of New Zealand" (2009 treaty) and/or "a resident of Thailand" (1989 treaty). A person
  **dual-resident in NZ and Thailand** can therefore appear eligible under **both**
  Australian treaties simultaneously — the treaties do not cross-reference each other.
- **Relief must be given by the residence state.** If NZ (domestic resident) and Thailand
  (remittance-basis resident) both tax, and the NZ–Thai treaty is needed to decide which
  one relieves the other, then Australia's own relief obligation (for directors'
  fees/dividends it may tax at source) sits on top of an unsettled residence question →
  **temporary double taxation and mismatched credit claims** are possible pending
  resolution/MAP.

## 6. Income-flow mapping matrix

Rows = income flow. Columns = residency outcome. Cells = **governing treaty + taxing-right
result**. All contingent on the residence and characterisation gaps below.

| Income flow | (a) Theo **NZ-resident** | (b) Theo **Thai-resident** |
|---|---|---|
| **Contractor / professional fees from HA Pty Ltd & T&H Collective (AU payers)** | **NZ–Australia 2009 DTA, Art 7 (Business Profits).** Taxable **only in NZ** unless Theo has a **PE in Australia** (Art 5). If services are performed remotely (Thailand/NZ) with no AU PE → **NZ sole taxing right; Australia cannot tax; no AU withholding on business profits.** | **Australia–Thailand 1989 DTA, Art 14 (Independent Personal Services)** (or Art 7 if characterised as enterprise profits). Taxable **in Thailand** unless Theo has a **fixed base regularly available in Australia** or exceeds the treaty **day threshold** in Australia → then **Australia may tax the attributable portion.** Thailand taxes on its **remittance basis** and gives relief. |
| **Directors' fees (IF Theo is a director of an AU company — unconfirmed)** | **NZ–Australia 2009 DTA, Art 15 (Directors' Fees).** **Australia (company's state) may tax**; **NZ** (residence) gives a credit. | **Australia–Thailand 1989 DTA, Art 16 (Directors' Fees).** **Australia may tax**; **Thailand** (residence) relieves (credit), subject to its remittance rules. |
| **Dividends (IF Theo is a shareholder receiving dividends — unconfirmed)** | **NZ–Australia 2009 DTA, Art 10 (Dividends).** **Australia** may levy **capped source WHT** (tiered; verify nil/5%/15%); **NZ** taxes with credit / imputation interaction. | **Australia–Thailand 1989 DTA, Art 10 (Dividends).** **Australia** WHT capped (**15% if ≥25% holding, otherwise up to 20%** — verify); **Thailand** taxes on **remittance** with credit. |
| **Any NZ-source income (e.g. NZ interest) while abroad** | Domestic NZ + (if Thai-resident issue) not applicable here. | **NZ–Thailand DTA** would govern (NZ source, Thai residence): e.g. interest capped at **10%** at NZ source (Art 11), Thailand relieves. *No such flow is evidenced.* |

**Cross-cutting notes on the matrix:**
- **6.1 Source question is itself unresolved.** Australia taxes non-residents on
  **Australian-source** income. Personal-services income is generally sourced **where the
  services are performed**. If Theo performs all work **physically in Thailand**, the fees
  may not be Australian-source under AU **domestic** law at all — in which case Australia
  has no taxing right *before even reaching the treaty*. Intake item 16 (any work done
  while physically in Australia) is the decisive gap. The treaty analysis above assumes,
  for completeness, that AU asserts source.
- **6.2 Characterisation is unresolved.** Whether each receipt is a contractor fee,
  director's fee, dividend, loan or reimbursement is **unknown** (intake item 15). The
  governing **article** changes with characterisation (Art 7/14 vs Art 15/16 vs Art 10).
- **6.3 PE vs fixed base — the thresholds differ.** NZ-residence routes services through a
  **PE** test (2009 Art 5/7); Thai-residence routes them through a **fixed base / day**
  test (1989 Art 14). A scenario giving no AU PE might still create an AU fixed base (or
  vice versa). This is a genuine, outcome-affecting divergence.
- **6.4 Remittance overlay (Thai-residence column).** Under Thai domestic rules (Por
  161/162 — the domestic-law agents' remit), Thai tax on foreign-source income can turn on
  **remittance** and the pre-2024 savings pool. Treaty relief interacts with, but does not
  remove, that overlay.

## 8. Open questions for advisers (cross-treaty)

1. **Permanent home (tie-breaker step 1).** Does Theo retain any NZ dwelling *available*
   to him (owned, leased, family home, stored belongings)? This can decide NZ-vs-Thai
   treaty-residence on its own — and thereby which Australian treaty frames all his income.
2. **Centre of vital interests.** How do personal ties (Phuket residence) weigh against
   economic ties (NZ sole-trader registration, NZ banking, Australian customers)? Needed if
   a permanent home exists in both states.
3. **Australian source and physical presence (intake item 16).** Is any work performed
   while physically in Australia, and how many days? If all services are performed in
   Thailand/NZ, Australia may have **no source-based taxing right at all**, short-circuiting
   both Australian treaties.
4. **PE / fixed base in Australia.** Does Theo have any fixed place of business, office,
   agent or habitual contracting presence in Australia? The answer runs through a **PE**
   test if NZ-resident (2009 Art 5/7) but a **fixed-base/day** test if Thai-resident (1989
   Art 14) — confirm both.
5. **Characterisation of each receipt (intake item 15).** Contractor fee vs director's fee
   vs dividend vs loan vs reimbursement — this selects the governing article and the
   source-country withholding outcome under whichever Australian treaty applies.
6. **Directorships and shareholdings.** Is Theo a director and/or shareholder of HA Pty Ltd
   or T&H Collective? If so, Art 15/16 (directors' fees) and Art 10 (dividends) give
   Australia source-taxing rights that the residence state must then relieve.
7. **Dual-resident relief chain.** If Theo is NZ domestic-resident **and** Thai
   remittance-resident, which state relieves which, and is a **MAP** request under the
   NZ–Thailand DTA (Art 25) needed to prevent double taxation while Australia taxes any
   directors' fees/dividends at source?
8. **MLI overlay.** Confirm the MLI-synthesised texts of both the 1989 (AU–TH) and 2009
   (NZ–AU) treaties for any principal-purpose test or modified tie-breaker that changes the
   analysis.
